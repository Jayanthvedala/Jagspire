                 The Role of AI in Modern Cybersecurity


AI is becoming a useful part of cyber security as the number of cyber attacks and the amount of security data continue to grow. Security teams have to deal with a large number of logs, alerts, network activities, and other events every day. Checking all of this manually can take a lot of time. AI can help by processing this information faster and pointing out activity that may need attention.

One of the main uses of AI in cyber security is detecting suspicious activity. It can look at normal patterns of user and system behavior and identify things that look unusual. For example, multiple failed login attempts, an unusual connection to an unknown system, or unexpected file activity could be flagged for further investigation.

AI can also be useful in a SOC (Security Operations Center). Analysts often receive many security alerts, and not every alert has the same level of importance. AI can help analyze and prioritize these alerts so that analysts can focus on incidents that are more likely to be serious. This can make the investigation process faster and more manageable.

Another area where AI can help is threat intelligence. Security teams collect information about malware, vulnerabilities, attackers, and different types of cyber threats from many sources. AI can help process this information and identify patterns that might otherwise be difficult to notice.

AI can also support incident response. During an incident, security teams need to understand what happened, which systems were affected, and what actions were taken. AI can assist with analyzing logs and other security data, helping analysts get a clearer picture of the incident.

In VAPT and application security, AI can also be useful for finding and analyzing potential vulnerabilities. It can help security professionals examine large amounts of application or testing data and identify areas that may require attention. However, the results still need to be checked by a security professional because automated tools can produce incorrect or incomplete findings.

AI is not only useful to defenders. Attackers can also use AI to make phishing messages more convincing, automate certain activities, and develop new ways of attacking systems. Because of this, organizations need to understand both the advantages and risks of using AI in security.

AI should therefore be treated as a tool that supports security professionals rather than replacing them. Human knowledge and decision-making are still important when investigating incidents, validating vulnerabilities, and deciding how an organization should respond to a threat.

Overall, AI is helping modern cyber security become faster and more efficient. It can assist with threat detection, alert analysis, threat intelligence, vulnerability identification, and incident response. When AI is combined with proper security practices and human expertise, it can become a valuable part of an organization's overall cyber security strategy.
